The Premise

The questions are the work.

Courageous, curious, sincere ones. The kind you can’t answer on autopilot — where the first response that arrives isn’t quite the true one, and the true one takes a moment to surface.

I’m not here to hand you a conclusion. I’m here to hold steady, open space while we work together to find yours, because the answers you arrive at yourself are the ones that hold.

Expanding your awareness and expanding your consciousness are the same motion. Everything else follows from there.

Who I Work With

You might recognize yourself here.

  • ·Something changed — a job, a move, a marriage ending — and you haven’t found your footing in the new version of it yet.
  • ·There’s a specific situation you’re carrying anxiety about right now.
  • ·You second-guess yourself in conversations that matter — and replay them afterward.
  • ·A breakup or an older relationship left a mark you’re still working around.
  • ·You give and give, and somewhere along the way stopped being able to say no.

Underneath a lot of this sits an old, quiet assumption about what we’re worth — usually picked up long ago, rarely examined since. Most of us are carrying some version of it.

Where We Usually Start

Three common areas of work.

These come up more than anything else — but they’re a starting point, not a menu. Most people arrive knowing something’s off without knowing where it lives. Finding that is part of the work.

01

Agency

Who’s been holding the wheel?

We hand our choices away in small pieces — to keep the peace, to be agreeable, to avoid a hard moment. Then something arrives and we find we’ve been driving from the passenger seat. The work is taking it back deliberately, before you need it.

02

Communication

Is the message sent the message received?

Almost never, and that gap is where most conflict actually lives. We look at what you meant, what landed, and what to do differently — using tools like Nonviolent Communication to make the invisible part visible.

03

Empathy

Can you extend to yourself what you give everyone else?

There’s the empathy that understands someone and the empathy that feels with them — knowing which you’re using changes what’s possible. And most people I meet are fluent in it outward and beginners at it inward.

The Method

Six moves, in order.

The sequence stays consistent. The tools inside it are chosen for you.

First

Arrive

We check where your nervous system actually is before we touch anything else. Nothing useful happens in a body that’s still braced.

Second

Open

You bring the thing. And if the thing is too hot to hold right now, we start somewhere softer and work toward it.

Third

Get curious

I ask about what you were reaching for underneath the action. Not to catch you out — to find out what you actually wanted.

Fourth

Find the gap

Between what you did and what you hoped it would get you. Seeing that gap clearly is usually the moment the courage to try something else shows up on its own.

Fifth

New eyes

Same facts, wider view. We set aside right and wrong in favor of a more useful measure: does this serve me, or doesn’t it?

Sixth

Practice

You leave with something to try — rehearsed while things are calm. Under stress we reach for what’s familiar, so the goal is to make the new thing familiar first.

How It Adapts

No single approach reaches everyone.

One person needs a reframe. Another needs to work through the body. Another needs the thing said in the language of the beliefs they already hold. I meet you inside your framework rather than asking you to adopt mine.

My Story

From engineering to this.

I came up in the structured, analytical world of engineering — systems, precision, problems taken apart until they made sense. Useful training. But over time I felt a pull toward working with people instead of parts.

So I studied: conflict resolution, communication, coaching. And I kept finding the same thing — the deepest changes didn’t come from new information. They came from someone seeing their own situation differently.

That became The Champagne Method: a practice rooted in empathy, intentionality, and the belief that every person holds the capacity for profound growth.

Then my path took a turn I didn’t plan. A heart attack stopped me cold, and the strain of it moved straight into my closest relationships. It could have been the hardest season of my life. It became the most honest one — an invitation to look inward and do the growth and healing I didn’t know I needed.

The tools I share aren’t theory. They’re what carried me through.

Champagne is my name. And the second fermentation — the one that actually makes the bubbles — happens sealed in the bottle, under pressure. That parallel wasn’t lost on me.

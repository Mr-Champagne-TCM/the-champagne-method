Projectors have no colored-in Sacral, and they're about one in five people. Their gift is seeing others clearly: how they work and what would help. Manifestors have a motor connected to the Throat but no colored-in Sacral. They're fewer than one in ten people. They're built to start things, on their own impulse, without waiting. Reflectors have no colored-in centers. They're about one in a hundred people. They take in and reflect everyone around them, which makes them a clear mirror for the health of a group or place. Emotional authority

Clarity comes over time, once the emotional wave has settled.

Emotional authority belongs to anyone whose Solar Plexus is colored in, about half of people. Feelings move in waves, so how you feel about a choice today may change tomorrow. The truth shows up as a calm, steady clarity after riding the wave. There's rarely truth in the heat of the moment.

A yes can feel exciting one day and flat the next. Neither is the final answer.

Over days, sometimes longer, a quieter feeling settles in. That steady feeling is the one to trust.

Big choices tend to go better with time: sleeping on it, sometimes many times. Small ones need less.

People around you may want a fast answer. "I'll come back to you" is a full answer.

Deciding at the top of a high or the bottom of a low. Both feel certain in the moment.

Waiting for perfect calm. Clarity here is a steady sense, not the absence of all feeling.

Other names you might see: Emotional is also called Solar Plexus. Ego is also called Heart. Self-Projected is also called G or Self. Some sites join type and authority into one label, so “Emotional Generator” means a Generator with Emotional authority.

Heart (also called Ego)

Willpower, promises and self-worth.

The Heart, also called the Ego, is willpower and self-worth. It's the part that makes promises and keeps them, and that knows its own value. It's also about the material world: money, work and getting paid fairly. It works hard, then needs rest.

You have steady willpower. You can make promises and keep them, and you tend to know your worth.

Your will works best with real rest between efforts. Pushing without breaks wears it down.

You can feel the willpower of people around you, and it may push you to prove yourself. You don't have anything to prove.

The trap is over-promising. Promising less, and only what you really want to do, tends to feel much better.

You have no steady willpower, so pushing yourself on will alone rarely works. You may feel you have to prove your worth, again and again.

Your worth isn't something to earn. Letting go of proving it is often a relief.

Split

Two separate groups of colored-in centers, with a gap between them.

In Split definition, your colored-in centers form two groups that don't connect. Nearly half of people have it. You may feel a gap, as if something's missing. Other people often bridge it, which can feel like they "complete" you. A small split is easy to bridge. A wide one takes more.

You may take a little longer to process things, as if two parts of you are catching up with each other. That's normal for you.

Time and conversation help the two halves meet.

People whose charts join your two groups can feel deeply familiar, or magnetic. That pull is real, and it's about the bridge, not always about the person.

Knowing this can make relationships calmer. Feeling complete with someone doesn't mean you're incomplete alone.

Other names you might see: “Split – Small” or “Split – Wide” says how far apart the two groups sit.
